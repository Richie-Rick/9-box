# Team Member Guide — Performance & Growth Cycle

Your walkthrough for the quarterly cycle: what to do, when, and what to expect, screen by screen.

## Before the cycle: your goals and key metrics

Open **Goals & Metrics** in the sidebar. You'll see two lists.

**Key metrics** are the recurring measures for your role, the numbers that describe what good looks like in your seat. Some are set by your manager, some you set yourself, and some are **Role-set**: attached to your job title by an admin and shared by everyone in that role. Role-set metrics can't be removed from your page; if one doesn't fit, raise it with your manager.

**Goals** are time-bound commitments agreed with your manager. A goal may span one quarter or several. You can add your own at any time (title, description, due date); your manager can add goals for you too. Each goal is labelled with who set it.

Before each cycle, check both lists are current. Aim for three to five key metrics and two to four active goals. If your page is empty, talk to your manager; agreeing them together is a useful conversation in itself.

## When the cycle opens

You'll get an email, and your **Dashboard** shows a banner for the open cycle with the three milestone dates and a **Start Self-Assessment** button. The dates matter:

- **Ready to Meet** is when your self-assessment needs to be submitted (your manager's assessment is due the same day).
- **Meeting Complete** is when your 1:1 should have happened.
- **Results Sent** is when the cycle closes.

The app emails a reminder on Sunday or Monday mornings for anything outstanding. If you're up to date you won't hear from it.

## Your self-assessment

Allow 30–45 minutes. You can save a draft at any point and come back; your manager sees nothing until you submit.

**Layout.** At the top right of the form is a toggle: **One at a time** shows a section per step with chips to jump between them; **All on one page** stacks every section with a menu down the side and a save bar at the bottom. Use whichever you prefer; the app remembers.

**1. Performance.** Rate your performance this quarter (1–3) and give the evidence. Below the questions is the **Goals & Key Metrics** panel. Do this part first:

- For each key metric, enter the **actual result** for the quarter, mark it **Hit**, **Missed** or **Unsure** against the target, add a comment if useful, and save. Your manager sees the same numbers.
- For each goal, record your progress: **On track**, **Off track** or **Complete**, with a note. This is *your* update; your manager records theirs separately and both show side by side.

Then describe your key achievements, the blockers or challenges you faced, and what you learned.

**2. Growth Readiness.** Rate how ready you are to take on more scope or a bigger role (Developing / Building / Ready Now), and say where you want to grow next and what shows you're ready. This is new for the self-assessment: your manager rates it too and you'll see both views afterwards.

**3. Values Alignment.** Rate yourself on each of the four values, Customer First, Step Into the Arena, Flock to Problems and Give Energy (1–3 each), then give specific examples. Each rating has a few prompts to consider.

**4. Engagement.** Rate how engaged you feel (1–3), what's driving that, and what support you need from your manager.

**5. Looking Ahead.** Add your goals for next quarter as tracked goals (title, description, due date). They're saved to your profile straight away. If you set goals last quarter in the old free-text box, they're shown here for reference.

**Submit.** The Submit button stays disabled until every rating is set; the form lists what's still needed and jumps you to it. Once submitted you return to the dashboard and your manager is emailed.

**Changing it afterwards.** You can re-open a submitted self-assessment from the dashboard (**Edit Self-Assessment**). Edits are recorded in a history you can see on My Reviews.

## Tips for a good self-assessment

**Be honest, not performative.** The value is in genuine reflection. If you had a rough quarter, say so; your manager will respect the self-awareness more than a polished version of events.

**Use specifics.** A deliverable, a client, a metric, a moment. "I did well" tells your manager nothing. "I turned around the BAS lodgements for 12 clients two days ahead of deadline" tells them everything.

**Let the metrics do some of the talking.** You've just recorded hit or missed against each one. Your performance rating should sit comfortably alongside that record.

**It's fine to rate yourself lower than your manager does.** Self-awareness is a strength.

**Submit before the Ready to Meet date.** Your manager can't share or meet until both assessments are in.

## Before your 1:1: read your review

Your manager rates you *without* seeing your self-assessment, then reads your answers section by section and writes a response to each. Once they've submitted, they **share** the review with you ahead of the 1:1. You'll get an email, your dashboard card shows **Shared**, and **My Reviews** shows:

- your placements on the two 9-box grids with the prescribed actions
- every section side by side: your rating and theirs, your comments and theirs, and their written response to what you said
- any goals and key-metric results for the quarter

Read it before you meet. The aim is a conversation about the gaps, not a reveal in the room.

If your manager changes a rating or comment after sharing, they have to give a reason, and you'll see an **Updated since shared** badge (and get an email) so you know to re-read.

## Your 1:1

This is a 45-minute structured conversation, the most important meeting of the quarter. Your manager runs it from the meeting page, which shows the same comparison you've already read, your goals and results, and a space for notes and actions.

**How it runs.** A human check-in first. Then you walk through your self-assessment; your manager listens and asks questions. Then they take you through their ratings and responses, leading with the rating before explaining it. Then the gap: where your views differ and why. You close by confirming the prescribed actions, any goal changes, and who's doing what.

**What to expect.** Leading with the rating can feel confronting the first time; that's normal, and it's about your output and behaviours, not your character. You're encouraged to disagree. If a rating doesn't feel right, say so and bring your evidence. Your manager will ask "does this feel fair?" and genuinely wants the answer.

**Actions.** Every agreed action is written into the meeting with an owner and a due date. They become tasks on your **Tasks** page, where you and your manager can both see and update them.

## After the 1:1

When your manager marks the meeting complete, their notes appear under **From your 1:1** on My Reviews and you'll get an email. They then **send results** to close the cycle out; your dashboard card shows **Review Complete**.

If anything on My Reviews doesn't match what was discussed, raise it with your manager. They can still add to their written responses after results are sent.

## Through the quarter

Keep your goals current from **Goals & Metrics**: update progress, add new ones, and record results when you have them rather than at the end of the quarter. Work your tasks from the **Tasks** page. Over time My Reviews builds into a history of your ratings, your manager's, and the gap between them. That history is yours; use it to own your development, not just to respond to feedback once a quarter.
