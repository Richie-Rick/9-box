# Setting up Goals and Key Metrics

Goals and key metrics are the inputs that ground every cycle. Results are recorded against them before anyone rates anything, so the 1:1 starts from shared facts rather than memory. This guide covers what goes where, who sets what, and how results flow into the review.

## What goes where

**Key metrics** are recurring performance targets for the role: the things you'd expect to look at every quarter (revenue retained, response time, on-time delivery, throughput). Set them once and revisit when the role changes. Each has a name, a target and an optional unit.

**Goals** are time-bound commitments agreed between a manager and a team member: specific things being worked towards this quarter or the next few. Each has a title, an optional description and due date. Closed when done, replaced as priorities shift.

Aim for **3–5 key metrics** and **2–4 active goals** per person. Fewer is usually better; if everything is a priority, nothing is.

## Who sets them

| Set by | How | Label shown |
|---|---|---|
| **The team member** | **Goals & Metrics** in the sidebar: add goals and key metrics for yourself | *Set by employee* |
| **Their manager** | **My Team → View Profile → Add Goal / Add Metric** | *Set by manager* |
| **An admin, for a job role** | **Admin → Roles & Templates**: attach metric and goal templates to a job title | *Role-set* |

Role-set items are created automatically for everyone with that job title (and for anyone who later joins it). They can't be removed or retitled from a person's page; changes are made on the template and flow to everyone. Notes and progress on a role-set item are still per person.

Everyone can see all three kinds on their own page; managers see them on the report's profile; both see them inside the assessment forms.

## What good looks like

**Good key metrics** are measurable, owned by the person, and reviewed every quarter:

- *Client revenue retained ≥ 95%*
- *Average response time to client queries < 4 business hours*
- *Engagement survey score for direct reports ≥ 4.0 / 5*
- *On-time delivery rate for quarter-end close ≥ 98%*

Avoid vague metrics like "be a good team player" or "improve communication"; those belong in the Values Alignment conversation.

**Good goals** are time-bound, specific, and have a clear finish line:

- *Migrate the top 10 clients to the new reporting template by 30 June*
- *Complete Xero advisor certification by end of Q2*
- *Reduce manual journal entries by 50% by 31 July via the automation rollout*
- *Hire and onboard two new associates by 30 September*

Avoid goals that are really "do your job". Goals should represent stretch, change or development, not BAU.

## Recording results during the cycle

Inside both assessment forms, the **Goals & Key Metrics** panel sits under the Performance section.

**Key metrics.** Enter the **actual result** for the quarter, mark it **Hit**, **Missed** or **Unsure** against the target, add a comment if useful, and save. Either the team member or the manager can record or correct a result; there's one result per metric per cycle. Managers can't move past the Performance section until every metric has a result.

**Goals.** Choose **On track**, **Off track** or **Complete** and add a note. The team member and the manager each keep their own update for the cycle, shown side by side; neither overwrites the other. A manager marking a goal Complete closes the goal; the team member's update is their own view.

**Why hit / missed matters.** The app compares the performance rating with the metric outcomes. A rating of Meeting or above with more metrics missed than hit raises a warning on the form and the summary, and shows under *Rating vs results* on Analysis. That's not a rule against it; it's a prompt to make sure the evidence (or the targets) are right.

## Setting goals for next quarter

The last section of the self-assessment, **Looking Ahead**, is where the team member adds their goals for the coming quarter as tracked goals. Managers add or adjust goals from the profile page, and should confirm the final set in the 1:1. Actions agreed in the meeting are tasks, not goals; if an action turns into a longer commitment, add it as a goal from the profile page.

## Through the quarter

Progress doesn't have to wait for the next cycle. From **Goals & Metrics** (team members) or the profile page (managers), update a goal's status, add notes, record results as they land, and add or close goals as priorities change. The employee dashboard shows the current cycle's goals and metrics at all times.

## Do this before the cycle opens

Get goals and key metrics in place **before the cycle opens**. The cycle-open email goes to everyone at once, and the self-assessment starts with recording results, so empty pages show up immediately.

If you're not sure what to load for someone, talk it through in your next 1:1. Agreeing on metrics and goals together is itself a useful conversation. For a standard set across a role, send the list to Pat to be set up as role templates.
